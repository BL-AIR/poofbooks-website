Literary fiction

Luminous Literature

Literary sounds all highfalutin, but it just means the characters take centre stage, not the plot. It’s about why a man does something, not what he does. Usually a slow build, man, that takes its time and trusts you to keep up. Two books tell you everything about this shelf. James Baldwin’s Giovanni’s Room, 1956: an American in Paris, a love he can’t hold on to. Brought me to tears the first time I read it. And André Aciman’s Call Me by Your Name: one Italian summer, first love, the peach and all. From those two right through to this year’s debuts. A good one leaves you knowing a little more about people. Peace, man.

Literary fiction for and about gay men: novels that take their time, trust the reader and stay with you long after the last page. Two books show the range: James Baldwin’s Giovanni’s Room, the 1956 classic of an American’s doomed affair in Paris, once passed hand to hand; and André Aciman’s Call Me by Your Name, the modern one everyone has read, one summer of first love on the Italian Riviera.
